| [How long early labor lasts](https://tallybump.com/tools/how-long-early-labor-last/) | Typical early-labor timelines, by birth history |

**The 5-1-1 rule in one line:** contractions **5** minutes apart, lasting **1** minute each, for **1** hour → time to head in (for a first birth; your provider may give different numbers for later births or high-risk pregnancies).

**How to time a contraction properly:** start the clock at the *first tightening*, stop at full relaxation — the duration is start-to-stop, and the interval is start-to-next-start. The [contraction timer](https://tallybump.com/tools/contraction-timer/) logs both automatically and keeps a history you can show your provider.

## 👣 Fetal Movement (Kick Counts)

| Tool | What it answers |
|---|---|
| [Baby kick counter](https://tallybump.com/tools/baby-kick-counter/) | Log movements and time your count sessions |
| [When to start kick counts](https://tallybump.com/tools/kick-counts-when-to-start/) | Which week to begin, and what a normal session looks like |

Most providers suggest starting daily kick counts around week 28. A common benchmark: **10 movements within 2 hours** while resting on your side. What matters most is the pattern — a change in your baby's usual movement style is worth a call, same day.

## 🩺 A Note on Medical Decisions

These tools are informational aids, not medical advice. Timing rules like 5-1-1 are common defaults — your own provider's instructions always win, especially for high-risk pregnancies, prior fast labors, or multiples. When in doubt, call. That's literally what the on-call line is for.
